About the Course

Course Description

This course is the second half of the department's two-course NLP sequence, picking up where classical statistical and structural methods leave off and tracing the path toward today's large language models. We move from syntactic parsing and the perceptron through distributional semantics and neural network architectures — convolutional and recurrent — to the attention mechanism and the transformer, the architecture behind every modern LLM.

From there the course turns to what makes a transformer into a large language model: subtoken encoding, positional embeddings, parameter counting and scaling laws, mixture-of-experts routing, post-training (instruction tuning and reinforcement learning), and reinforcement learning more generally. Two quizzes, four homeworks, and a final project — proposed, implemented, and presented — anchor the term.
